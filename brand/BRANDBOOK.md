**Mark version:** V2 (August 2026)
**Status:** Official. Use for all merchandise, print and digital.
**Next revision:** V3 will add the dancing couple to the reserved slot in the
dome. Nothing else about the mark will change, so V2 assets stay valid until

---

## 1. The mark

A medallion. A banded dome over a dark banner carrying the wordmark, flanked by
a tumbadora at left and a conga pair at right, over a fan of seven root loops
with a Cuban tres at lower left and a clave pair at lower right.

Every element is drawn geometry. Nothing in the mark is a photograph, a
generated image, a stock illustration, or type set in a font.

### What the mark means

**The roots are the argument.** Tradition below, name above, growing out of it.
That is the one element doing unambiguous symbolic work, and it is why the
composition holds.

**The instruments are the pedagogy.** Three conga-family drums, a tres placing
the mark in the son lineage rather than generic "Latin," and clave — the
instrument the teaching is built on and the first thing in the curriculum
sequence.

### What the mark does not mean

Read this before anyone else asks you about it.

- **No rhythm is encoded anywhere.** The mark contains 13 dots in the red band,
  11 teal segments and 11 sunburst rays. Every one of those counts came from
  dividing an arc by a minimum feature width so nothing fell below the print
  floor. There is no 3-2, no 2-3, no cinquillo. If someone reads clave into
  those counts, they are reading arithmetic.
- **Nothing sacred appears in the mark.** Every instrument shown is secular.
  Clave replaced an earlier shekere partly for this reason: the chekeré carries
  güiro and bembé association, and clave carries none.
- **The palette was sampled, not chosen.** It was measured out of the original
  2024 artwork and regularised into flat colours. Red and white appear together
  but as part of a nine-colour scheme, not as a dyad.
- **The corner flourishes carry no scattered dots.** The 2024 file had three
  loose dots at each upper corner, at different sizes and positions on each
  side. They were decoration the generator produced with no role in the
  composition, and they are gone. If you see them in an old asset, that asset
  is stale.

colours, not text colours.

## 3. Typography

**Every letterform in the mark is drawn.** There is no font to license, embed,
or supply, and no possibility of a substitution on a vendor's machine.

**"ROOTS OF"** — custom caps on a Havana Art Deco skeleton: geometric,
circular bowls, high waist, with slightly flared terminals so they read as
painted signwriting. Deco in Havana is the 1920s–40s, the danzón and charanga
era, so the register matches the period the curriculum centres.

**"Salsa"** — a brush script where thickness is a function of stroke direction:
heavy on the pull stroke, light on the push, with entry and exit taper. One
shear is applied to the whole word, so the slant cannot drift.

### For supporting material

The mark carries no companion typeface, because none is needed inside it. For
flyers, decks and the website you need one, and the safe pairing is:

- **Headlines:** a geometric sans — Poppins SemiBold or Montserrat SemiBold.
  Both are free and echo the drawn caps without competing with them.
- **Body:** the same family at Regular, or any neutral serif at 16 px minimum.
- **Never** set supporting text in a script. One script per layout, and the
  logo already used it.

## 4. Sizing and clear space

### Clear space

Minimum clear space on all four sides is **one cap height of "ROOTS OF"**,
which is **7% of the mark's width**. At a 3.5 in mark that is 0.25 in.

Nothing enters that zone — no text, no rule, no photo edge, no other logo.

### Minimum sizes, measured

These were established by rendering the mark down and looking, not asserted.

| Use | Minimum width | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Screen, full detail | **160 px** | everything reads |
| Screen, acceptable | **128 px** | drum hardware and clave soften |
| Screen, absolute floor | **96 px** | wordmark still legible, detail is mush |
| DTF / garment transfer | **3.5 in** | what the artwork was engineered for |
| Screen print | **1.6 in** | assumes a 0.5 mm floor |
| Offset / digital paper | **1.25 in** | legibility governs before the press does |
| Embroidery | **do not use** | see below |

**Embroidery needs a different mark.** Stitch cannot hold features below about
1.5 mm, which at this artwork's proportions would demand a 4.9 in patch. For
hats and jacket backs, commission a simplified embroidery version — likely the
dome and wordmark only, instruments dropped.

## 6. Correct use

**Do:**
- Place the colour mark on cream, white, or any light ground
- Place the white knockout on black, dark brown, or any dark ground
- Scale proportionally, from the vector wherever possible
- Respect the clear space

**Do not:**
- Stretch, skew, rotate, or reflect the mark
- Recolour it, including "just making it all one colour" — the white knockout
  already exists and was designed as a separate reduction, not a recolour
- Add drop shadows, glows, strokes, bevels, or gradients
- Place the colour mark on a busy photograph — use the white knockout with a
  scrim, or a plain panel
- Place the colour mark on a mid-tone ground where the ink brown stops reading
- Reproduce below the minimums in section 4
- Reconstruct the mark from a screenshot or a traced raster

### The one palette rule that matters

The nine colours travel together. **Do not produce a reduced two-colour version
in red and white.** In the full mark, red sits among teal, orange, amber and
brown and reads as part of a scheme. Isolated as a dyad it reads differently to
anyone inside the tradition, and that is not a conversation you want a flyer to
start on your behalf. Reduce to the white knockout instead — that variant
exists precisely so you never need to invent one.

## 7. Production notes

The artwork was built to a **1 mm minimum feature floor** at 3.5 in wide,
covering both printed strands and unprinted gaps that show the garment.

Verified by eroding the artwork by half the floor: any region that vanishes
entirely is a genuine sub-floor strand. Regions that merely shrink are tapers
and corners, which print fine.

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Ink below 1 mm | 0.013 % of ink area |
| Unprinted gaps below 1 mm | 0.005 % of gap area |
| Ink coverage | 30.4 % |

For reference, the original 2024 artwork had **74.7 %** of its dark linework
below 1 mm and **38.8 %** below 0.5 mm.

The SVGs contain flat filled paths only. No strokes, gradients, filters, masks,
clipping paths, transparency or embedded rasters — nothing that requires a RIP
to interpret anything unusual.

## 8. Reserved: the dancing couple

The dome interior is deliberately empty. It is not an oversight and it is not
a background — it is a reserved slot, and nothing else in the composition
depends on that region.

Brief for the illustrator:

- **Slot:** roughly x 300–740, y 60–440 in the mark's 1000-unit space. Feet may
  run below y 416, where the banner covers them.
- **Colour:** silhouettes in `#2A1210`, the woman's dress in `#9E2A18`.
- **Keyline:** cream `#F6F0DA` separation of 15 units (1.33 mm at 3.5 in)
  around the outside of the pair.
- **Floor:** no ink strand and no cream gap below 12 units (1.07 mm).
- **Hands:** resolve as tapered terminals, not fingers. A finger at this size is
  about 0.9 mm.
- **Pose:** open break — man leading from the left, woman's outer arm raised,
  dress flaring right. Joined hands must **overlap**, not merely approach.
- **Deliverable:** flat filled vector, no strokes, no gradients.

For the white knockout, supply the couple as a single silhouette shape. It will
be unioned in with a 12-unit garment-coloured separation ring, the same
treatment the drums, tres, clave and wordmark already receive.
